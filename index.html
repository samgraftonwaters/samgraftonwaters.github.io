Sam Grafton-Waters

Hi, I'm Sam and I have just finished my PhD from the Mullard Space Science Laboratory where I studied the environments around supermassive black holes at the centre of galaxies, called active galactic nuclei, or AGN. I explore the outflowing winds of AGN with X-ray data collected from XMM-Newton with the main aim of understanding how the black hole and galaxy co-evolve with each other.

About Me

I have just completed my PhD at the Mullard Space Science Laboratory in the heart of the Surrey Hills, where I was under the supervision of Prof. Graziella Branduardi-Raymont and Prof. Mat Page . Before this, I did an integrated Masters degree in Physics with Astrophysics at the University of Leicester .

My interests are in research, data analysis, and problem solving. I have run outreach projects for school students to explore real X-ray data of AGN and have their worked published, organised seminars, and presented my research at conferences.
